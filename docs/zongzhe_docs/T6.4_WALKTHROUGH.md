# T6.4 walkthrough: tuning when two sightings are the same object

**What this is.** A plain-language guide to what T6.4 did and the code it added, in the order it
happened. Each step points at the code. Written 2026-10-22, branch `t6.4-merge-tuning`. Owner:
Zongzhe. Read [`T6.3_WALKTHROUGH.md`](T6.3_WALKTHROUGH.md) first: it explains the six steps from one
photo to objects, and T6.4 is about the last one.

## The problem

The robot sees the same chair in many photos. Each photo gives a **sighting** of it. The memory
should hold **one** chair, not one per photo. Step 6 of the T6.3 walkthrough (S10 and S11) decides
this: for each new sighting, is it an object already stored, or a new one?

It decides with a score:

    score = 1.5 x overlap + 0.5 x appearance        (phys_bias 0.5)
    merge if score > 0.6                            (sim_threshold)

- **overlap**: the fraction of the new sighting's points that have a stored object's point within
  the **match radius**. Upstream used 2.5 cm.
- **appearance**: how alike the two CLIP number lists are (step 4 of T6.3).

`combined_scores` and `match` in [`association.py`](../../memory_graph/association.py) (lines 112 and
117), `overlap_matrix` at line 61.

If the rule is too strict, one chair becomes several objects: **duplicates**. If it is too loose,
two different things become one object: a **false merge**. T6.4 finds the setting with the fewest
of both.

## Step 1: an answer key (T6.4e)

To count mistakes we need to know what is really in the room. You marked every piece of furniture
on the drive's LiDAR map: 16 objects, each with a class, an outline on the floor and a height range.
The file is [`memory_graph/eval/lab_ground_truth_handdriven1.json`](../../memory_graph/eval/lab_ground_truth_handdriven1.json),
explained in its [`README.md`](../../memory_graph/eval/README.md).

The glass bookcase is flagged `many_parts`: the graph sees each of its cells as a separate "shelf",
and those should not count as duplicates.

## Step 2: run the expensive part once

SAM 3 and CLIP need the lab GPU and take about 2 s per photo. The merge settings come after them,
so they only need running once.

[`run_drive.py`](../../memory_graph/run_drive.py) goes through the whole drive:

1. Skips a photo unless the camera moved 25 cm or turned 15 degrees since the last kept one
   (`anchor_gate.pose_moved`). The real gate is 1 m or 45 degrees. Saving more photos than that lets
   a replay try other gate settings.
2. Runs SAM 3 and CLIP on the photo, and saves it with their output (`save_photo`, from T6.3).
3. Marks whether the real gate would keep the photo too.

It saves a CLIP vector for **every raw detection**, not only the cleaned ones. `features_from_raw`
(line 53) finds each cleaned detection's vector by its label, score and box. So a replay with other
filter settings still gives every object its own vector.

`handdriven1`: 103 photos, 26 kept by the real gate when applied to those 103. (Applied to every
frame, the real gate keeps 29. The 26 are a close stand-in.)

## Step 3: the scorer

[`score_drive.py`](../../memory_graph/score_drive.py) replays the saved photos on any computer, no
GPU, through steps 3 to 6 of T6.3 plus the clean-up (`replay`, line 203). Then it compares the
stored objects with the answer key (`score`, line 128).

**Placing a graph object on a real one.** Its box centre must be inside the real object's outline
(grown by 20 cm) and within its height range (grown by 30 cm). The height test stops a TV on the
wall from landing on the sofa below it. The label does not have to match: a "table" on a marked
desk is placed there, and the label is counted separately.

Then, for each real object:

| Count | Meaning |
|---|---|
| duplicate | more than one graph object of its kind on it (desk and table count as one kind, so do shelf and cabinet) |
| part | the same, but on the bookcase flagged `many_parts` |
| miss | no graph object on it at all |
| false merge | one graph object whose points cover two real objects (points inside two marked boxes do not count, since they cannot tell them apart) |
| extra | a graph object of a furniture class that lands on no real object |

Only the 13 furniture classes are scored. Books, lights, paper and so on stay in the graph but are
not counted.

## Step 4: the sweep

A scratch script (`docs/zongzhe_docs/local/t6.4/sweep.py`, not in git) ran the scorer with one
setting changed at a time, then with several together. Baseline at the real gate: **8 duplicates,
0 false merges, 4 misses**.

What came out:

| Change | Result |
|---|---|
| match radius 2.5 to 10 cm | 8 duplicates to 3, no new misses |
| less weight on overlap (phys_bias 0) or a low threshold (0.3) | 0 duplicates, but only about 35 objects left and 10 to 14 of the 16 real objects missed: everything merged |
| more weight on overlap | more duplicates |
| one-to-one matching | 13 duplicates, worse than greedy's 8 |

**Why the radius.** LiDAR depth covers only 12 to 14 percent of the pixels, and two photos of the
same object put it 4 to 9 cm apart (T6.3). With a 2.5 cm radius, two sightings of one chair rarely
have points that close, so their overlap is near 0 and they stay apart.

**Over-merging hides in the misses column.** When everything merges, one huge object spreads its
points over many real objects, so no single one holds enough of them for the false-merge rule.
The real objects then show up as missed.

## Step 5: checking on a second drive

A setting tuned on one drive might only suit that drive. So a second drive (2026-10-21) went
through steps 2 and 3 too. Its map was lined up with the first by trying every heading and fitting
the walls (`docs/zongzhe_docs/local/t6.4/align_maps.py`, scratch): it started 1.2 m away and 3.6
degrees turned. 81 percent of its map cells landed within 10 cm of the first map's. Your 16 objects
were moved into its frame.

On the second drive a larger radius also cut duplicates (2 to 1). But at 15 cm, chair 4 merged into
the desk beside it. At 10 cm it stayed a chair. So: **10 cm**.

## Step 6: the decision

[`config.py`](../../memory_graph/config.py): `match_radius_m` is now 0.10. The weights stay
upstream's. Recorded as D-MG2 in [`MEMORY_GRAPH_DESIGN.md`](../MEMORY_GRAPH_DESIGN.md): the merge
test stays **geometry-dominant**, because more weight on appearance merges everything.

A guard test, `StoredPhotosTest` in [`test_score_drive.py`](../../memory_graph/tests/test_score_drive.py),
replays the three stored photos and checks nothing merges that should not.

## What is still open

- Two drives, 16 objects, a handful of duplicates. A difference of one is noise.
- kappa (how many photos to keep per object, D-MG1) was not checked on the drive.
- A D455 depth stream (dense) may want a smaller radius.
