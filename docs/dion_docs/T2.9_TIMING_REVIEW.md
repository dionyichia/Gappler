# T2.9 timing review: what has to line up for a gaze pick

**Main point:** a gaze pick works only if three things come from the same moment: the gaze
samples, the glasses image, and the moment you meant the object. Today the client never knows when
any of them really happened. It only knows when each one *reached it*, and it guesses the gap with
fixed numbers. This doc walks through every player, every delay and every knob, then gives a
senior-engineer review of the design at the end (section 9).

**Branch:** `t2.9-aria-detection-on-recording`, PR #76. Written 2026-10-17 from a fresh start.
The older file-by-file guide is `T2.9_REVIEW_GUIDE.md`. The interactive timeline is the published
page "Gaze Pick Timing" (https://claude.ai/artifact/ATTdi1xvAerASf6PXYN8Wy). It still shows the values before 2026-10-17 (knob 0.65, drift not fixed): use its "1.5, late image + fix b" preset for today's code.

**How to use it:** one section per tutor lesson, in order. Each section only needs the ones before.

---

## 1. The players, in order

A "player" is one program or device that touches the signal. Nine of them, from your eyes to the
arm.

| # | Player | What it does | Sends |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aria glasses | Record RGB camera, eye cameras and microphones, stream them over USB or Wi-Fi | raw frames, raw audio |
| 2 | `rgb_worker` (in `image_streaming_pipeline.py`) | Undistort, then rotate the RGB frame (T2.13), JPEG it | `/aria/rgb_camera/undistorted` |
| 3 | `et_worker` (same file) | Run the eye model on the eye camera image, turn gaze into a pixel of the published image | `/aria/eye_tracking/gaze` (a bare `Point`) |
| 4 | `audio_worker` (`audio_streaming_pipeline.py`) | Wait for you to stop speaking, then Whisper turns speech into text and a small LLM picks the object word | `/aria/audio/prompt` (a bare `String`) |
| 5 | **`aria_segmentation_client`** (this PR) | Joins word, gaze and image. Sends the word at once, then asks SAM 3, picks the mask you looked at, sends a second message with that object's crop | `/grasp/target` (twice), `/manipulator/grasp_request` |
| 6 | `segmentation_service` | Runs SAM 3 on one image with one word | a reply with masks and scores |
| 7 | `grasp_segmentation_client` | Segments the **wrist** camera with the word, picks the best score | `/object_centroid_2d`, `/camera/sam/mask` |
| 8 | AnyGrasp detector | Finds grasp poses on the chosen mask | `/grasp_candidates` |
| 9 | Grasp state machine | Moves the arm, only after a grasp request opened its gate | arm commands |

Nav nodes (`object_approach_node`, `goto_glasses`) also listen to the word on
`/aria/audio/prompt` [code, CHANNEL_CONTRACT H1].

**This PR changes players 2, 3 and 5**, plus the service's default settings (6) and the grasp
client's request (7). The grasp side ignores the second message's crop today: that is T8.5.

## 2. Two kinds of time

Every signal has two times:

- **Capture time:** when the glasses saw or heard it. This is what you care about.
- **Arrival time:** when it reached the client. Always later, by an amount that varies.

The client uses arrival time for everything [code]:

| Signal | Time the client uses | Where | Carries a capture time? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaze | `time.monotonic()` when it arrives | `aria_segmentation_client.py:99-100` | No. `Point` has no header |
| Image | `time.monotonic()` when it arrives | `:89-90` | Its header is stamped at publish, after undistort (`ros_publisher.py:33`), not at capture |
| Word | `time.monotonic()` when it arrives | `:119-120` | No. `String` has no header |

So every delay below has to be guessed with a fixed number. That is the root of most of this
review. (The pose pipeline already stamps with the glasses' own capture time,
`pose_streaming_pipeline.py:266-271`, so the codebase has done it once.)

## 3. Delay 1: from your voice to the word

You finish saying "pick up the box". The word reaches the client about **1.5 s later** [inferred,
measured on a recorded command, `docs/bench-runs/2026-10-15-labbox-t2.9-review-fixes.txt` item 3].

| Step | Time | Knob or source |
|---|---|---|
| Glasses send the audio | unknown, not measured | none |
| Wait for the next check of the audio | 0 to 0.25 s | `iteration_interval_s` 0.25, `aria_config.yaml:77` |
| Wait for quiet that ends the sentence | 0.7 s | `end_of_speech_silence_s`, `aria_config.yaml:86` |
| Voice detector padding | 0.4 s | faster-whisper default, not in config (`audio_streaming_pipeline.py:101-105`) |
| Whisper, then the LLM | about 0.4 s | GPU speed |

**What the client does about it:** it assumes you spoke `speech_latency_s` before the word
arrived, and ends the gaze window there (`aria_segmentation_client.py:132`). The knob is 0.65 s.
The measurement says about 1.5 s. Choosing the value is open decision **a** (section 8).

**One more catch:** "when you spoke" is ambiguous. "Pick up the box" takes about 1.7 s to say.
Did you look at the box at the start, at the word "box", or at the end? Section 9 comes back to
this.

## 4. Window 1: the gaze window

**What it is:** the client averages gaze over the last `gaze_window_s` (1.0 s) before "when you
spoke", because eyes never hold still (`gaze_pick.py:6-9`). At 10 gaze samples a second that is 10
samples. On the box it was 4.5 a second, so about 4 samples [measured].

```
time  -->
gaze  . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .
            [=== 1.0 s window ===]
                                 ^ arrival - speech_latency_s     ^ word arrives
```

**Then the pick** (`gaze_pick.py:12-21`): the mask under the averaged gaze point, else the nearest
mask within `pick_radius_px` (80 px). Recording c2cbafe8 needed about 60 px at 1.4 m.

**What can go wrong:**

- Window too early (knob too large): it averages gaze from before you looked at the box.
- Window too late (knob too small): it includes the moment you looked away, at the robot or at
  the next thing.
- A glance between two objects averages to a point between them, on neither.

## 5. Window 2: the image

**What it is:** the client keeps only the newest glasses image (`:90`). When the word comes:

1. No image ever: send the word alone (`:123-125`).
2. Image older than `gaze_window_s` (1 s): it may not show what you looked at, so wait up to
   `image_wait_s` (3 s) for the next one (`:127-131`). `on_image` then segments it (`:91-97`).
3. Otherwise: segment it now.

**Image rate:** the replay sent one image every 1.2 to 3.1 s on the box [measured]. Live rate on
the box is unknown [unverified]. This is why the wait exists.

**The drift (open decision b):** after a wait, `segment` runs again from `on_image`, and `now` is
the new image's arrival time (`:121`). The gaze window is then measured from *that* time, up to 3 s
after the word, not from the word. Fix: save the word's arrival time in `on_prompt` and use it in
`segment`. About 2 lines.

**The mismatch even without drift:** with the knob at 1.5 s, the gaze window is 1.5 to 2.5 s
before arrival. The image is at most 1 s old. So the image is always newer than the gaze it is
compared with, by 0.5 s or more. If your head turned in between, the gaze pixel points at a
different part of a different picture.

## 6. Delay 2: the SAM 3 call, and stale replies

**What it is:** the call takes about 0.25 s on the wrist images [code comment,
`grasp_config.yaml:39`]. For the larger glasses image it was not measured [unverified].

During the call you might speak again. The client counts every word in `self.words` (`:107`). Each
request remembers the count it started with (`:144-145`, the lambda captures it). A reply whose count is old is dropped
(`:148-149`). So "box", stop, "box" cannot bring back the first reply.

This is the one timing problem in the client that is fully solved.

## 7. After the pick: the two messages and the arm

Each word makes, in order:

1. `Target(prompt="box")` on `/grasp/target`, at once (`:108-111`).
2. `Empty` on `/manipulator/grasp_request`, at once (`:113`). This opens the state machine's gate
   for `grasp_request_timeout_s` (10 s, `grasp_config.yaml:115`).
3. Later, after the image wait and SAM call: `Target(prompt="box", reference=crop,
   reference_mask=mask)` (`:163-167`).

**Why the order matters:** the grasp side starts acting on message 1, with the best SAM score in
the wrist image. Message 3 arrives 0.3 to 3.5 s later. Today that is harmless, because the grasp
client ignores the reference (`grasp_segmentation_client.py:92-95`). Once T8.5 makes it use the
reference, the arm may already be moving toward the wrong box when the right crop arrives.

**Other grasp-side timers:** `segment_timeout_s` 5 s gives up a wrist request whose reply never
came (`grasp_config.yaml:40`).

## 9. Senior review: how this is usually done, and what we have not considered

Written as a robotics and vision engineer would review it. These are for you to think about, not
to-dos. None is decided. Ranked by how much each would change the result.

### 9.1 Use capture time, not arrival time (the big one)

**How it is usually done:** every sensor message carries the time it was captured, in
`header.stamp`. Readers keep a short history of each stream, indexed by time, and look up the
sample nearest the moment they need. In ROS this is what `message_filters` and `tf2`'s buffer do.
Stream-processing people call it "event time vs processing time".

**What it would replace here:**

- `speech_latency_s` (a guess) becomes a measured time per sentence. `audio_worker` already knows
  when the sentence started and ended (`audio_streaming_pipeline.py:175-182`), and Whisper already
  returns **word-level timestamps** (`word_timestamps=True`, `:135`). The time the object word was
  said is one subtraction away.
- `image_wait_s` and the drift go away. Keep the last few images (a few MB) and pick the one taken
  inside the gaze window, instead of the newest one.
- Gaze becomes `PointStamped` with the eye camera's capture time.

**Cost:** a contract change on three topics (`String` to a stamped message for the word, `Point` to
`PointStamped` for gaze, image stamp at capture). About 30 to 60 lines across the workers and the
client. Breaks the nav nodes that read `/aria/audio/prompt` unless the stamped word goes on a new
topic. **Clock note (Dion, 2026-10-17):** word, gaze and image all come from the glasses, so
stamped with the glasses' own capture time (`capture_timestamp_ns`) they compare with each other
directly, and no clock offset is needed for the pick. An offset to the host clock matters only
when glasses data meets host data (wrist camera, arm, nav). It can be estimated as the smallest
`host arrival - device capture` seen over a few seconds, which leaves only the fastest transport
delay as error [inferred]. With stamps, a slower pipeline only delays the answer. It no longer
makes the three signals come from different moments.

### 9.2 Gaze leads speech: anchor on the noun, not the sentence end

**What psycholinguistics says:** when people name an object, their eyes land on it roughly 0.5 to
1 s *before* they say its name, and often leave it right after (the "eye-voice span", Griffin and
Bock, 2000 [from memory, not checked here]). In "pick up the box" the noun is last. In "that box,
pick it up" it is first. A window tied to the end of the sentence hits the first case and misses
the second.

**Option:** with Whisper's word times (9.1), end the gaze window just after the object word's start.

**Also common:** people look at the robot, the listener, while giving a command. The window should
prefer gaze that sits on a candidate object, not the mean of everything.

### 9.3 Vote by dwell time instead of averaging

**Problem with a mean:** gaze at box A for 0.6 s and box B for 0.4 s averages to a point between
them, which may be on the table.

**How it is usually done:** fixation detection (group gaze samples that stay within about 1 degree
for over 100 ms) or a vote: for each gaze sample, which mask is it on? The mask with the most
samples wins. It also gives a confidence: 9 of 10 samples is sure, 5 of 10 is ambiguous and could
trigger the clarifying question (T7.4).

**Cost:** about 10 lines in `gaze_pick.py`, testable offline on c2cbafe8 with the existing tool.

### 9.4 Gaze is a ray, and the image camera is not your eye

**The geometry:** the eye model gives a direction, not a point. The RGB camera sits a few
centimetres from your eyes. So without knowing the depth, the gaze is a **line** in the image, not
a pixel. The code cuts the ray at a fixed 1.5 m (`default_depth_m`) to get one pixel.

**Correction (independent review, 2026-10-17):** the 60 px offset at 1.4 m is **not** from the
fixed depth. Cutting at 1.5 m instead of 1.4 m moves the pixel only about f x b x (1/1.4 - 1/1.5)
= 609 x 0.06 x 0.048, about 2 px [inferred, eye-to-camera baseline b not measured]. The 60 px
(about 5.6 degrees) is a bias in the eye model or its calibration. Depth only matters up close: at
0.5 m the same sum gives about 50 px. So the line pick below helps for near objects, and a per-user
calibration is what would remove the 60 px.

**Options, cheapest first:**

- Accept the line: pick the mask the projected gaze line crosses, between say 0.5 and 4 m. This
  removes `default_depth_m` from the pick.
- Depth from the eyes' vergence (both eyes' angles meet at a depth). Noisy past about 1 m.
- Depth from the scene: the glasses have SLAM cameras. Hard.

**Also:** a radius in pixels means a different size at each distance. Eye trackers state their
error in degrees (about 1 to 2 for Aria [unverified]). 80 px at a 609 px focal length is about 7.5
degrees, which is generous.

### 9.5 The real hard problem is the handoff to the robot's view

The glasses pick a mask in **your** view. The arm grasps in the **wrist camera's** view, from a
different place and later. T8.5 plans to match the crop by appearance.

**How others do it:**

| Approach | Good at | Fails at |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance match (T8.5) | Different-looking objects | Two identical boxes. Lighting and viewpoint change |
| Shared 3D map: gaze ray in the map frame, using the glasses' pose (MPS or our SLAM) and the robot's map | Duplicates. Gives nav a goal too | Needs both devices localised in one map, about 5 to 10 cm error to beat |
| Both: 3D to choose the region, appearance to confirm | Most cases | Most work |

The 3D route also fixes NEXT_STEPS 2.13 (the robot cannot always see what you see) and T7.4 (go
back to navigation along the gaze ray). Worth deciding before T8.5 is built.

### 9.6 Do not let the arm act before the pick is in

Section 7: the grasp request and the word-only Target go out at once, the reference later. When the
reference starts to matter, the usual pattern is one of:

- Send the grasp request only after the pick, or after a timeout with the word alone.
- Make the grasp side wait a short time for a reference after a new word.

Today this costs nothing to fix, because the grasp side ignores the reference. Later it decides
whether the arm picks the box you looked at or the box SAM 3 likes most.

### 9.7 Smaller things a reviewer would raise

1. **Blinks.** The eye model returns a guess during a blink. Real systems drop low-confidence gaze.
   `et_worker` publishes every estimate [inferred, not checked whether the model gives a
   confidence].
2. **A decision record per word.** Log, or save to a file, the image stamp, the gaze samples used,
   every mask with its score and distance, and the pick. Tuning on real use needs this more than
   any knob. Today only one log line survives (`:168-169`).
3. **Tune knobs on data, not by hand.** The offline tool already replays c2cbafe8. A sweep over
   `speech_latency_s`, `gaze_window_s` and `pick_radius_px` on a few labelled recordings, with the
   right object marked, gives a number for each choice. One recording of a steady stare cannot
   choose them.
4. **Live rates.** Image and gaze rates on the box were 0.5 and 4.5 a second with the replay, and
   the box throttles its CPU (T2.15). Every window above assumes more. Measure on the live glasses
   before tuning.
5. **Continuous "what am I looking at".** The opposite design: segment a few images a second all
   the time, keep a history of which object you fixated, and look it up when the word comes. No
   waiting, but costs the GPU all the time. Usually not worth it until the on-demand way is shown
   too slow.

### 9.8 What I would do, if asked

1. Decide b now (2 lines, removes a real bug).
2. Set a to 1.5 s, the measured value, because a wrong guess is worse than a known gap. Then record
   the gap as a reason for 9.1.
3. Add a task for 9.1 plus 9.2 (stamped messages, word-time window, image history). It replaces a,
   b and `image_wait_s`.
4. Try 9.3 offline on c2cbafe8. Cheap, and it is already the tool's job.
5. Decide 9.5 and 9.6 before T8.5 starts.

### 9.9 Gaze optional, and memory for time-indexed caches (Dion's questions, 2026-10-17)

**Gaze optional:** the word alone already stands when there is no gaze or no pick
(`aria_segmentation_client.py:133-135`, `:157-161`). With dwell voting (9.3), "you were not
looking at any candidate" becomes a measured case: no mask holds enough of the window's samples.
That adds one human-behaviour knob (the smallest share of samples that counts as looking). The
catch: without gaze the grasp side takes the best SAM 3 score, which on c2cbafe8 was the wrong box
(write-up finding 3). Gaze optional needs a fallback that is not "highest score": ask (T7.4), or
the 3D route (9.5).

**Glasses image rate:** both recordings on the Mac use `recording_profile: profile0` (their
`.vrs.json`), which records RGB at 1 a second (119 frames in 118 s, 94 in 95 s) [measured]. Live
streaming uses `profile18` (`aria_config.yaml:62`), whose RGB rate was never measured here
[unverified]. So 1 a second is the recording's rate, not the glasses' limit. Measure the live rate
with `ros2 topic hz` at the next glasses session.

**Memory, 20 s of history per stream** [inferred, box has 31 GiB RAM, 27 free, GPU 16 GB, read
2026-10-17]:

| Stream | Per sample | Rate | 20 s | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glasses RGB, JPEG | about 0.3 MB | 10 a second if live allows | about 60 MB | keep JPEG, decode only the one used |
| Gaze | 24 bytes | 10 a second | under 10 KB | already a 200-sample deque |
| Audio | 7 mics, 48 kHz | continuous | about 25 MB [inferred, 32-bit samples] | already kept, `max_buffer_s` 15 |
| Wrist RGB + depth, raw | about 1.5 MB | 30 a second | about 900 MB | **do not cache.** The grasp side needs only the newest frame. Arm poses at a past time come from tf2's buffer, which already keeps 10 s |
| Base camera | about 3.5 MB raw | 30 a second | about 2 GB raw | **do not cache the stream.** Nav keeps keyframes, about one per 1 to 2 s (HiCo-Nav cascade, PAPER_REPORT 4.4a) |

RAM is not the limit if each cache is sized to its need. GPU memory and the box's throttled CPU
are (T2.15, ORIENTATION's VRAM note). Each reader keeps its own small buffer. No shared cache
service.

### 9.10 Independent review (2026-10-17)

A fresh reviewer with none of the discussion read the code and the proposal (9.1 to 9.9).
**Verdict: the direction is sound, but too much for PR #76.** What it added:

1. **Head motion.** A gaze pixel only matches the image taken at the same moment. Gaze from 1.3 s
   around the word belongs to several head poses, but the vote runs on one image. So: pick the
   image first, then use only gaze samples close to that image's capture time [inferred]. Today's
   mean has the same flaw.
2. **Word timing is more work than it looks.** Whisper's word times are relative to the sentence
   clip. The clip's real start comes from assuming the buffer ends "now"
   (`audio_streaming_pipeline.py:174-175`). The glasses' audio timestamps are thrown away in
   `AudioObserver.on_audio_received` (`audio_streaming_client_observer.py:51`) [code]. Also, the
   audio replay plays a separate WAV, not the VRS audio, so it shares no clock with the image
   replay (`replay_audio_file_to_prompt.py`) [code].
3. **One clock convention.** The pose pipeline already stamps with raw device time, no offset
   (`pose_streaming_pipeline.py:218-220`, `:266-271`) [code]. Host-converted stamps elsewhere would
   give two conventions. For the gaze pick alone, device time is enough.
4. **Factual corrections:** the 60 px is not from depth (9.4). Gaze has two more readers that a
   `PointStamped` change must update (`visualizer/ros_manager.py:79`,
   `object_recognition_pipeline.py:159`) [code]. The image's capture time exists and is dropped in
   `rgb_worker` [code]. The eye model returns uncertainty bounds that the code drops
   (`aria_device/eye_tracking.py:128-142`) [code]. `aria_config.yaml:107` "10 eye frames" is out
   of date on the box.
5. **Simpler first:** English commands usually end with the object, so "end of sentence" is close
   to the object word. Voting can reuse `pick_mask` per gaze sample. One Target after the pick (or
   a timeout), then the grasp request, is simpler than two Targets. `message_filters.Cache` is
   ROS's standard time-indexed buffer [unverified in Humble's Python].

Its order for the follow-up: measure live rates first, then stamp image and gaze, then the image
history, then voting near the image time, then the stamped word, then the ordering change with
T8.5. Radius in degrees, the line pick and the uncertainty only if recordings show a need.
