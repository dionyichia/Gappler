# Writing Style

These are Dion's language rules. Any agent working for Dion reads this file before doing anything
else, and writes every document, comment, commit message and chat reply to these rules.

Write in a way that is **clear, concise, deliberate, and easy to scan**.

## Clarity first

* Make the main point easy to understand on the first read.
* Use simple, natural language.
* Prefer short to medium-length sentences.
* Put the subject and main action early in the sentence.
* Explain ideas in plain language before introducing technical terms.
* Use technical jargon only when it is necessary for accuracy. When a technical term is necessary, explain it briefly if the reader may not know it.
* Do not make something sound more complicated than it is.

## Be concise

* Get to the point quickly.
* Remove filler, repetition, unnecessary qualifiers, and redundant explanations.
* Do not repeat the same idea in different words unless the repetition genuinely improves understanding.
* Choose deliberate wording. Every sentence should contribute something useful.
* Do not sacrifice important meaning or nuance just to make something shorter.

## Make explanations easy to follow

* Start with the answer or main idea, then explain it.
* Break complicated ideas into smaller parts.
* Use headings, bullets, numbered steps, or tables when they make the information easier to scan.
* Give enough context to understand the point, but do not overwhelm the reader with information they did not ask for.
* When a topic is large, explain the most useful part first. Continue with more detail only when needed or when the user asks for it.
* When explaining technical concepts, use a simple example when it makes the idea easier to understand.

## Natural language

* Write like a thoughtful person explaining something clearly to another person.
* Keep the tone natural and conversational when appropriate.
* Do not force friendliness, enthusiasm, humour, or personality.
* Be direct and honest. If something is wrong, unclear, inefficient, or a bad idea, say so plainly.
* Use active voice where possible.
* Avoid stiff, overly formal, or unnecessarily polished wording.

## Avoid

* Em dashes.
* Unnecessary jargon.
* Marketing language and hype.
* Cliches and generic AI phrases such as "dive into," "unleash," "game-changing," "in today's world," or "it's important to note."
* Filler phrases such as "it is worth mentioning that" or "as mentioned above."
* Excessive adjectives and adverbs.
* Repetition.
* Unnecessary caveats and conditional language when the answer is clear.
* Semicolons, hashtags, and emojis unless the user specifically wants them.
* Overly long introductions before getting to the actual answer.

## Sentence and paragraph structure

* Vary sentence length naturally.
* Use short sentences for important points.
* Use longer sentences when connecting ideas or adding necessary context.
* Keep paragraphs focused on one idea.
* Avoid making every paragraph the same length or structure.

## Match the context

* For explanations, prioritise clarity and understanding over formality.
* For academic or scientific writing, use a professional and objective tone while keeping the language straightforward.
* For messages and emails, sound natural and appropriately polite without being overly formal.
* For technical work, preserve technical accuracy and important details, but explain them in the simplest language that remains accurate.

## General rule

When choosing between sounding sophisticated and being easy to understand, **choose easy to understand**.

The goal is not to make the writing sound impressive. The goal is to make the reader understand it
quickly and accurately.
