## 2. Weak directions along the route `[measured]`

`degeneracy.py`: every 5th scan, normals of the session's own accumulated map at the scan's points (frozen T3.5e settings: 0.10 m voxel, 12 neighbours, planar points only), then the eigenvalues of the mean of n n^T over matched points. The trace is 1, so a value is a share of the information. 0.33 would be isotropic horizontally plus vertically. About 2,300 points per scan are matched.

| Run | Smallest horizontal eigenvalue: median / 10th percentile / minimum | Scans below 0.11 | Weak direction (10 / 50 / 90th percentile, world angle, 0 = +x) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Run 1 | 0.128 / 0.101 / 0.084 (t = 58 s, x -0.7, y 0.0) | 14 % | 81 / 93 / 164 deg |
| Run 2 | 0.127 / 0.106 / 0.090 (t = 35 s, x 1.9, y 0.3) | 19 % | 47 / 88 / 148 deg |
| `handdriven1` | 0.136 / 0.122 / 0.111 | 0 % | 15 / 54 / 154 deg |

Answers to the question:
- The mid-room loop has a weak direction, but it is **near y, not x**, in both runs (median about 90 deg). `[measured]`
- It is mild. The middle horizontal eigenvalue is about 0.17 and the smallest 0.13, a ratio of 1.3. Nothing like the condition number of about 22 seen for a 3 s query in T3.5e. `[measured]`
- `handdriven1` avoids the worst of it (minimum 0.111 against 0.084 and 0.090) and its weak direction rotates, so no single direction stays under-constrained. That is a 30 % difference in the worst case, not an order of magnitude. `[inferred]` It cannot by itself turn 12 mm into 0.3 m.
- The weakest stretches sit beside the walls at the left of the room (x -1 to 0, y 0 to 1) and at the lower right (x 2, y -1 to -2). See `route_report_delta0.png`, top row.
- Counting only points inside the glass box changes the numbers little for runs 1 and 2 (median 0.128 against 0.128 and 0.132). Glass phantoms are not what holds the weak direction down.

## 3. Independent reference track `[measured]`

`reference_track.py`: 1 s windows every 3 s of each run, placed with the run's own poses and registered to the `handdriven1` map with the frozen T3.5e prototype (levelled sessions, 4-DOF, FLAT_MAX 0.5, previous solution as the initial guess, 0.9 inlier gate). Error = FAST-LIVO2 position minus reference position, zero at the first window. The map was built from `handdriven1`'s own poses, so the reference carries that map's error (about 7 cm against the tape in T3.5e). Every window passed the gate (inliers 0.92 to 0.997), so none was dropped. Reference and FAST-LIVO2 end within 1 to 15 mm of each other.

| Pairing of scan stamp and pose | Run 1: max / median error | Run 2: max / median error |
|---|---|---|
| Pose at the scan stamp (the prototype's convention) | 0.118 m at t = 39 s / 0.043 m | 0.111 m at t = 36 s / 0.030 m |
| Pose at stamp + 0.15 s | 0.058 m at t = 55 s / 0.011 m | 0.074 m at t = 37 s / 0.016 m |

With the plain pairing the error rises from t = 20 s to a peak of 0.11 to 0.12 m between 36 and 57 s and falls back to under 1 cm when the robot stops. The error vector points **against the direction of travel** (cosine -0.99 and -0.96 with the velocity) and equals about 0.22 to 0.25 s times the speed. `[measured]` That is the signature of a time offset, not of drift: drift would not vanish at rest.

`time_offset_sweep.py` tests it without any map. Moving 2 s windows are placed with the pose at stamp + DELTA for a sweep of DELTA, and the thickness of surfaces is measured. A wrong offset smears walls by speed times error.

| Bag | Best DELTA, median (quartiles) | Surface thickness at DELTA = 0 / at the best |
|---|---|---|
| Run 1 (13 windows) | +0.15 s (0.15, 0.20) | 6.4 mm / 5.5 mm |
| Run 2 (16 windows) | +0.15 s (0.15, 0.16) | 6.8 mm / 5.5 mm |
| `handdriven1` (30 windows) | +0.15 s (0.15, 0.15) | 7.5 mm / 6.2 mm |

All three recordings agree. `[measured]` The curve has a 0.1 s sawtooth because there is one pose per scan, so read the median, not a single window. Reading `[inferred]`: the odometry message stamps are the publish time of the mapper (the harness notes say all three source publishers stamp `node->now()`), about 0.1 s after the sensor time on top of the end-of-scan convention. It is a stamp convention, not an estimation error, and with it removed the reference agrees with FAST-LIVO2 to 1 to 2 cm in the median and 6 to 7 cm at worst, which is the reference's own error. **Where along the route:** with the pairing corrected there is no stretch where the error accumulates and stays.

Consequences `[inferred]`:
- Anything that builds a map from `/aft_mapped_to_init` plus the scans by nearest stamp (the T3.5e relocaliser prototype did) smears surfaces by about 4 cm at 0.28 m/s. Small, but it is the cause of the "doubled wall" look at speed, not glass alone.
- It is not a cause of the closure error.

## 4. Does the error line up with weak directions, glass or turns? `[measured]`, small samples (about 24 intervals)

Rank correlation of the change of error per 3 s interval with route features, after the +0.15 s correction (before it, in brackets):

| | Glass distance | Yaw rate | Weak-direction share | Mean abs cosine of error change with weak direction (0.64 = unrelated) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Run 1 | -0.33 (-0.51) | +0.60 (+0.84) | -0.28 (+0.09) | 0.67 (0.68) |
| Run 2 | -0.30 (-0.26) | +0.42 (+0.84) | -0.14 (+0.11) | 0.74 (0.67) |

- **Weak directions:** no relation. The error change is not aligned with the weak direction (0.67 and 0.74 against 0.64 for unrelated). `[measured]`
- **Glass:** the 30 to 58 s stretch, where the error is largest, is the part of the route within 1.2 m of a glass plane (100 % of run 1's near-glass scans, 74 % of run 2's). After the correction its mean error is 0.028 and 0.041 m against 0.006 and 0.010 m outside it. So some residual error sits beside glass, but it is 3 to 4 cm, inside the reference's own 7 cm. `[inferred]` Not distinguishable from reference error.
- **Turns:** the correlation with yaw rate drops from +0.84 to +0.60 and +0.42 once the time offset is removed. A turn raises both the time-offset error (the sensor moves faster) and, plausibly, the reference's smear. Not separable here.

## What to do with this

1. The A, B, C replays still have value as a harness and compute check, but do not expect them to separate suspects 1 and 2 on closure: the displacement is in the input. A better first use is the 0.02 m check in section 1's last paragraph, on the live system, no driving.
2. For the closure story, drive a loop and put a physical stop on the start pose (wheel chocks or a taped footprint with a bump stop), then compare the LiDAR-only start-to-end displacement with the tape. If they agree and odometry agrees, drift is not the issue.
3. The 0.15 s pose-stamp offset is worth one line in the T3.5e relocaliser notes and a decision about whether the bridge should restamp. Not done here.
