## Answer

**There is no 0.3 m drift to locate.** `[measured]` Three independent readings put run 2's start on run 1's end and run 2's end on run 1's start, so each loop
ended 0.32 m from where it started, in opposite directions (the P/Q swap of the cause note). Against the handdriven1 reference map, FAST-LIVO2 stays within
6.7 cm in both runs for the whole route (12.7 cm if the pose stamp lag is not removed) and within 1 and 12 mm at the end. The two runs' maps agree to
2.6 cm median. What is left is a stamp lag that scales with speed, and it vanishes at rest.

Inputs I had to settle: the brief's seed for 0b was left as a template (`<dx, dy, heading>`), so I used the global sweep. The replay copy of handdriven1
(`bags/hd1`) has no poses or `/cloud_registered`, so I used the original bag under `t35d-attended-20261020.raLTgE/run/bag/handdriven1`. Step 4 is the
earlier `degeneracy.py` output (same recipe, same scans) re-plotted in the common frame, not recomputed. The stationary bags carry no mapper poses, so step 6
is sensor-level. The base tracks are `p_imu - R c` with the bridge constant, and the mark readings are shifted by the run 1 base start because the bridge zeroes
odom at the start base pose `[inferred]`.

## Report

1. **Where the runs diverge.** Not from each other and not from the reference beyond 7 cm. The 0.32 m is placement: run 1 starts at P and ends at Q, run 2 starts at Q and ends at P, which is why the closure errors are equal and opposite. `[measured]` Which end is the taped A is `[reported]` by Sherman's recollection in the cause note (run 1 started on A). The only divergence along the route is the stamp lag error, from t = 20 s (motion starts) to the slow-down at 57 to 60 s, and it does not accumulate.
2. **Weak segments, glass, wall distance, turns.** Over all windows the error follows speed (Spearman +0.75 run 1, +0.61 run 2), which is the lag. It also correlates with glass distance (-0.60, -0.65), but glass distance and speed are confounded: the start and end are far from glass and stationary. Over moving windows only (n = 36 and 38): glass distance -0.25 and -0.25, weak-direction eigenvalue -0.41 and -0.24, yaw rate -0.41 and -0.50. All of this is on errors of at most 6.7 cm, at the reference's own error, so these are indications and not causes. Turn-induced translation is at most 2.5 cm mid-turn and about 1 cm per 360 deg in the corrected configuration, and heading-dependent, so it does not add up across a loop that returns to its heading (the loops turn +359 deg and return within 0.8 deg).
3. **Did handdriven1 avoid those regions.** Not in plan view: its track covers the left stretch (x -1.9 to -1) where runs 1 and 2 have a minimum. Its worst eigenvalue is 30 % better (0.111 vs 0.084 and 0.090) and its weak direction rotates. That cannot turn 12 mm into 0.3 m, and with no drift in runs 1 and 2 nothing needs turning. `[inferred]`
4. **Ranked explanation.** (1) Placement, the P/Q swap, `[measured]`. (2) Pose stamp lag of about 0.15 s, `[measured]` three ways (route note, step 3, step 5b), explains the speed-proportional error and not the closure. (3) Turn-coupled error, at most 1 cm of the 0.32 m. (4) Weak direction and glass, not supported. The extrinsic sign is weakly in favour of the corrected value (13.5 vs 16.0 mm) but the precorrection spin was also faster (10.8 vs 9.4 deg/s) and each is a single spin, so it is not decisive. `[inferred]`

## What the replays still need to decide

- Replays A, B and C take the recorded input, which already contains the 0.32 m displacement. Their closure number will be about 0.3 m by construction and says nothing about drift. Judge them by the step 3 metric instead: dump the replay poses, run `step3.py` on them against handdriven1, and compare the maximum error with 6.7 cm. A visual-update pull (B) or glass phantoms (C) would show as an error above that, or as a tilt of the replay track away from the live track.
- D (handdriven1 at rate 0.3) tests whether the estimator reproduces the 12 mm closure when it has four times its time budget, and so separates compute starvation from everything above.
