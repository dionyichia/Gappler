# T3.5e restart-in-map localisation: start, 2026-10-20

Status: started. Offline feasibility only. Nothing here is a localisation result. T3.5e is not
passed. No robot time used. Not pushed.

## The premise (confirmed from the pinned source `[code]`)

The pinned FAST-LIVO2 fork only writes a PCD (`pcd_save.pcd_save_en`, saved on shutdown to
`Log/pcd/all_raw_points.pcd`). Nothing reads one back, so "restart in the map" needs an external
relocaliser that finds the transform from a fresh session's `camera_init` frame to the saved
map's frame. The bridge in T3.5f would then publish `map -> odom` from it. T3.5e therefore decides
what has to wrap the mapper, as its task text says.

## Decision: version 1 is option A `[decision, Sherman, 2026-10-20]`

The robot restarts near a known pose. An operator-supplied guess is refined by registration. Start
anywhere (global relocalisation) is version 2 and out of scope here.

## What was tried (offline, from the T3.5d bags; no robot, no mapper rerun)

`tools/fastlivo/reloc/reloc_offline_prototype.py`: build a map by transforming the hand-driven
bag's LiDAR scans with the mapper's own odometry (no deskew, no loop closure), voxelise at 0.10 m,
then register the first 3 s of the spin-corrected bag (a separate mapper launch, started at a different
position from the tape mark, per Sherman, attended T3.5d, 2026-10-20) against it with point-to-plane ICP in numpy and scipy. Only `extrinsic_T` is applied;
`extrinsic_R` is identity in the mapper config `[config: t35d config line 27, T3.5_LIDAR_IMU_EXTRINSIC_FINDING.md]`.
No Open3D or small_gicp on the box. Runs 1 to 3 are the first session; runs 4 to 7 (below) are
the T3.5e follow-up. Box run directory `~/rcp-Gappler/log/t35e-reloc/` (needs `install_nav` sourced).

### Correction to the first write-up

The first version said "the odometry-accumulated map is about 5 cm thick". That was wrong. The
5 cm figure is the point-to-nearest-point rms against a 0.10 m voxel map. That spacing alone gives
roughly 5 cm at a perfect fit, so it is the voxel floor, not a measure of the map. The
point-to-plane residual is not capped that way: it is 1.3 to 2.6 cm in the self-tests below
`[measured on this prototype]`. Map thickness is not otherwise measured `[inferred at most]`.

## Placement follow-up (T3.5e A, runs 9 to 20): the 1.2 m figure was wrong

Verdict, one of three: **placement difference real.** The two sessions did not start at the same pose. The
corrected offset of the spin-corrected start, in the hand-driven map frame, is about **x 1.94 m, y 1.19 m, yaw
within 1 deg** (2.3 m in plan) `[measured on this prototype]`. It is a LiDAR-geometry result with one
tape-measured check (see "First scored check" below), and no accuracy statistic.

### Why I now say real, not aliased or undetermined

The criteria I used: one dominant best fit well clear of the rivals, the same answer when the query length and the
filter change, and the camera agreeing on direction.

| Query | Filter | Polished origin pose (x, y m; yaw deg) | Inliers | Plane rms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| first 3 s | on | 1.94, 1.20; +0.07 | 0.975 | 2.7 cm |
| first 3 s | off | 1.94, 1.18; -0.90 | 0.974 | 2.5 cm |
| first 15 s | on | 1.93, 1.19; -1.16 | 0.959 | 3.0 cm |

`[measured]` (runs 16 to 18). The three agree within 1.5 cm and about 1.3 deg. The earlier sweep best,
(0.18 to 0.42, 1.22), scored 0.73 to 0.75 inliers and 3.9 cm, and every other distinct solution scored 0.70 or less
`[measured]` (runs 9, 13). A session against its own map scores 1.00 and 1.3 to 2.6 cm, so the new fit is close to
that quality. How I got there:

1. **Degeneracy.** At the old sweep best, `A^T A` (the 4-DOF least-squares curvature) had its weakest direction almost
   pure x (0.92 to 0.99 of tx), eigenvalue 0.010 against 0.95 for the strongest, condition 90 to 100, with the
   planarity filter on. 96% of the kept normals were vertical (floor and ceiling), so walls, which fix x and y, hardly
   entered. `[measured]` (runs 9, 10). With the filter off the condition is 17 to 18 and 10% and 13% of the normals
   point along x and y (run 13). x was the aliased axis.
2. **y fit profile** (`results/placement/yprofile_*.csv` and `.png`): for each y of the query centroid, x and yaw
   re-optimised, 14 starts including the sweep's top solutions. The profile is rough with several local dips
   (e.g. y = -1.8, -1.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.6), but its best point is a clear winner: 0.975 inliers against 0.65 to 0.84
   elsewhere. The profile's y is the query centroid, not the origin, which is why the best row (y = -1.7) is the
   same pose as the origin y = 1.2 after converting (runs 14 to 18). I misread that conversion at first. `[measured]`
3. **Global sweep with a 15 s query** (run 15): the sweep still missed the best solution (its starts only reach 1.5 m)
   and put x at 0.2 to 0.4, y 1.2 to 1.6. The profile-then-ICP route found the 0.959 solution above. So the sweep needs
   wider offsets (this is a fix to the prototype, not to the data).
4. **Camera first frames** (`results/placement/f0_first.png` hand-driven start, `f1_first.png` spin-corrected start):
   same scene, shelf plus TV plus sofa, but the shelf is about 1.8 times wider from the second start, and moves from
   left of centre to right of centre. The camera looks along LiDAR +x (third row of `Rcl` is [1, 0, 0]) and
   +y is left, so "closer to the shelf and shifted left" matches a start 1.94 m forward and 1.19 m left in direction.
   `[inferred]`. I did not check the magnitude: the focal length was not used and a rough estimate gave about
   0.75 m of lateral shift, not a clean match. So the camera says "the views differ and the direction fits", nothing more.
5. **Raw range check** (run 11, first 1 s of each bag, 10th-percentile range by azimuth): inconclusive. The bins are
   30 deg apart with 15 deg windows, so they leave gaps, and the hand-driven start has something 0.5 to 0.9 m behind
   and to the left (probably the operator). Not used for the verdict.

### Version-1 design requirement: an inlier-fraction acceptance gate `[measured basis]`

The relocaliser must not publish a pose unless the registration passes a gate on the **inlier fraction** (share of query
voxels with a map point within 0.15 m after the fit). Provisional threshold **0.9**, to be set from the three or more
floor-mark pairs. Below the gate it reports failure and publishes nothing: no `map -> odom`, no pose.

Basis, all on this map with the 3 s query (about 12,000 voxels) `[measured]`:

| Case | Inlier fraction |
|---|---|
| true solution (runs 16 to 18, 21, 26, 27) | 0.959 (15 s query) to 0.975 (3 s) |
| wrong local fits (runs 4, 9, 13, 24, 29) | 0.73 to 0.75 |
| other distinct sweep solutions | 0.70 or below |
| option-A start that did not converge (run 26) | 0.713 |

The gap between the two groups is about 0.2, so 0.9 sits in a wide clear band. Limits: one room, one map, one query pair;
the value depends on the query window length, the 0.10 m voxel and the 0.15 m inlier radius, so it must be re-set if
those change. Inlier plane rms (2.5 to 3.0 cm for the true fit, 3.7 to 3.9 cm for the wrong ones) separates the groups
too, but is not part of this requirement.

## Prototype status: frozen, 2026-10-20

The numpy prototype is frozen as of this note: no more prototype features. It answers what it was built to answer
(`reloc_offline_prototype.py` and the `reloc_*` check scripts, runs 1 to 31, with the default `FLAT_MAX 0.5`). Failing from
identity with a true offset of 2.3 m is the expected result for option A, whose working range is about 1.0 m and 20 deg.
A global search from anywhere is option B and is not attempted. The sweep grid in `reloc_offline_prototype.py` was
deliberately not widened.

## Build path: option 2 (hybrid) is decided

Decided in the T3.5e session on 2026-10-20 (instruction in the session). Two options were on the list:

| | Option 1: a 3D relocaliser | Option 2: the hybrid (decided) |
|---|---|---|

## Option 2 build, Phase 1 design checks (2026-10-20, offline, nothing started)

Wiring: FAST-LIVO2 gives `odom -> robot_base_link` through a bridge. SLAM Toolbox keeps `map -> odom` and `/map`,
reading `/scan` made from the LiDAR cloud. Nothing here edits the task tree or `CHANNEL_CONTRACT.md`; that waits for Dion.

**1. CustomMsg to PointCloud2 `[measured]` `[code]`.** The Livox driver publishes one format per run
(`lddc.cpp:169-173`, `584-596`): nav uses PointCloud2 (`lidar_only.launch.py:19`, `xfer_format 0`), FAST-LIVO2 needs
CustomMsg (`lidar_type 1`, T3.5 consumer contract). Searched the repo, the driver and the fork: no converter exists. The
"owned PointCloud2 wrapper" in the T3.5 notes is `lidar_only.launch.py`, which only selects the format. So a converter node
is needed for nav's `/scan` path in fastlivo mode: it replaces `qos_relay`, subscribes `/livox/lidar` (CustomMsg) and
publishes `/cloud_relay` (PointCloud2, best effort). It must not use a normal rclpy subscription: decoding one
19,968-point CustomMsg takes **110.6 ms** in rclpy, more than the 100 ms period. A raw subscription decoded with numpy
takes **0.38 ms** and matches rclpy field for field on all 60 test messages (header, `timebase`, every point's x, y, z,
reflectivity, offset time; `reloc/` probe, box, `handdriven1`). CustomPoint is 20 bytes per point after CDR alignment.

**2. Twist and `/odom` `[measured]` `[code]`.** `/aft_mapped_to_init` carries no twist: all 2,356 messages have an
all-zero twist and zero covariances. The source sets the pose only (`LIVMapper.cpp:1559-1575`). Its stamp is the node clock
at publish time (`:1561`), not the LiDAR time. Rate on `handdriven1` is 20 Hz in visual mode (min gap 6 ms, max 98 ms), not
the 10 Hz of the T3.5f review. Decision: **`/odom` stays on the wheels**. Nav2 reads velocity from `/odom`
(`nav2_params.yaml:14,200`) and the mapper has none; the wheel driver publishes `/odom` whether or not `pub_tf` is on
(`ros2_interface.cpp:206-232`, TF only if `pub_tf`). The bridge's optional `/odom` is off by default: if turned on it would
derive velocity from pose differences, which is noisier than the wheels. One wrinkle: the vendor launch that starts
`xnode_vehicle` (`bringup_basic_ctrl.launch.py`) does not pass `pub_tf`, and the parameter is read once at start, so
`odom_source:=fastlivo` needs an owned copy of that launch with `pub_tf: False`. The vendor folder is third party and is not edited.

**3. The bridge transform `[code]`, tested `[measured]`.** Written in the module docstring and tested in
`bench/test_fastlivo_odom_bridge.py` (6 tests, stdlib, added to `bench/run.sh` as an L0 step). With `P(t)` the mapper's pose of
the IMU body in `camera_init`, `P0` the first pose and `Tb` the body in the base (offset
(0.191, 0.02329, 0.15588) m = `lidar_mount` (0.18, 0, 0.2) minus the corrected `extrinsic_T`, identity rotation):

`T_odom_base(t) = Tb * P0^-1 * P(t) * Tb^-1`. At startup this is the identity: odom is the base at startup, level.
The tests check: the offset arithmetic; the first sample is the identity; a straight drive moves the base along its own
forward even when it starts rotated 30 degrees; turning in place about the base centre leaves the base still;
a 0.73 degree plus -0.4 degree tilt of `camera_init` cancels, so a level circular drive keeps z, roll and pitch at zero;
wrong frame names are refused. Four deliberately broken versions (offset sign, no lever arm, no startup reference, the old
wrong offset) all fail the tests. SLAM Toolbox is already set to `odom_frame: odom`, `base_frame: robot_base_link`,
`transform_timeout: 0.5` (`slam_toolbox.yaml:10-25`), so a 20 Hz `odom -> robot_base_link` is enough.

### Phase 1 review items (2026-10-20): wheel TF, stamp latency, gravity

Scripts and output in `tools/fastlivo/bridge_checks/`. Box, isolated domain 177, no hardware, nothing commanded.

**Wheel TF: `GroupAction` plus `SetParameter` reaches the driver only if the group is unscoped `[measured]`.** The vendor launch
starts `xnode_vehicle` inside a 15 s `TimerAction`. With stub nodes that declare `pub_tf` as the driver does (default true, read
once) and the unmodified vendor `bringup_basic_ctrl.launch.py`: a scoped group left `pub_tf` True (`ros2 param get`, and the
stub's own log), identical to the no-`SetParameter` control. A node started at once in the same scoped group read False (the
harness works), and `GroupAction(scoped=False)` around the vendor include read False. The scope closes before the timer fires.
No copied vendor launch is needed. Cost of unscoped: the global parameter also reaches every node launched after it; only the base
driver declares `pub_tf`, other nodes ignore it or hold an inert extra parameter.

**Stamp latency on `handdriven1` `[measured]`, `[code]`.** The mapper stamps odometry and its TF with the node clock at
publish (`LIVMapper.cpp:1561`, `:1574`); the commented text on that line is the upstream alternative. LiDAR messages arrive
0.101 s after their header stamp (the stamp is the scan start). Source scan = latest scan arrived before the odometry:

| Odometry stamp minus | p50 | p95 | p99 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| source scan header stamp (start) | 124.0 ms | 191.7 ms | 199.0 ms | 202.0 ms |
| source scan end (start + 0.1 s) | 24.0 ms | 91.7 ms | 99.0 ms | 102.0 ms |

Two odometry messages per scan (2,356 for 1,179 scans): the first after each scan, p50 17 ms and p95 26 ms after scan end; the
second, p50 32 ms and p95 96 ms. The pose is stamped 24 ms (median) to 92 ms (p95) after the moment it describes. At 0.5 m/s
that is a 12 mm to 46 mm lag, and at 30 deg/s a turn lag of 0.7 to 2.8 deg when SLAM Toolbox looks the pose up at the scan stamp.
Minimal patch, not made: the pose time is `LidarMeasures.last_lio_update_time` (set at `IMU_Processing.cpp:437`, already used as
the pose time in the evo output at `LIVMapper.cpp:583`), so changing `:1561` to
`odomAftMapped.header.stamp = rclcpp::Time(static_cast<int64_t>(LidarMeasures.last_lio_update_time * 1e9));` stamps the pose and
the TF (`:1574` reuses it) with the measurement time (scan end, or image capture time for the visual update), removing the lag.
One line, but it needs a rebuilt image (sudo Docker) and a check that those times are in the same clock domain as the scan stamps
in the live run (the replay bag suggests yes). Open for approval.

**Gravity `[code]`, `[measured]`.** In the pinned fork the IMU init sets the state gravity from the mean accelerometer
reading and `rot_end = I` (`IMU_Processing.cpp:147-148`), so by default camera_init is the initial IMU frame, not gravity-up.
`LIVMapper::gravityAlignment()` (`:342-356`) rotates the state so gravity is `(0, 0, -g)`, but only when
`uav.gravity_align_en` is set: default false (`:89`), set true in our T3.5 config (`t35d_contrast_precorrection_visual.yaml:97`,
identical in the stationary02 pair), and all four mapper logs of the attended session print "Gravity Alignment Finished". So
camera_init is aligned to the accelerometer's idea of up, and is only as good as that estimate. Measured on the T3.5d starts (floor
plane fit in camera_init, first 3 to 10 s, radius 3 to 5 m): `handdriven1` floor tilt -0.36 to -0.63 deg about x and +0.15 to
+0.50 deg about y; `spin-corrected` +0.54 to +1.18 deg about x and -0.03 to -0.33 deg about y. The floor-tilt spread
between the two sessions (about 1.2 to 1.5 deg) matches the relative roll the registration needed (1.46 deg), and each floor tilt has
the same sign as that session's first-pose roll (-0.73 and +0.73 deg). Reading `[inferred]`: for a robot that starts level, the
first-pose tilt is the accelerometer alignment error, so camera_init is off-level by about the first-pose tilt.

Decision for the bridge: **P0 is the full first pose** (odom equals robot_base_link at startup, including roll and pitch), not a
levelled yaw-plus-translation pose. Residual floor tilt in the odom frame after removing the first-pose tilt: -0.36..-0.63 + 0.73 =
+0.10 to +0.37 deg for `handdriven1`, +0.54..+1.18 - 0.73 = -0.19 to +0.45 deg for `spin-corrected`, against up to 1.2 deg if
camera_init were trusted as is. What the tests assume: the robot starts level on level floor. The "level circle" test builds
camera_init tilted by an arbitrary constant (0.73 and -0.4 deg here) and checks the tilt cancels; it does not assume
camera_init is gravity-up. A startup tilt guard is built into the node: it refuses to publish if the first pose's roll or pitch is
above `max_startup_tilt_deg` (provisional 2 deg; measured 0.73), because beyond that the robot was not level or the alignment
failed. Evidence is two sessions; the guard is the safety.

## What a credible version needs

1. A better map: the mapper's own saved PCD (deskewed, voxelised) from a replay of the
   hand-driven bag with `pcd_save_en` on, written to the bind-mounted `Log` directory.
2. A better registration: GICP or NDT (PCL 1.12 is installed on the box) with a coarse global
   initialisation (yaw sweep plus scan descriptors) so an arbitrary start can work.
3. Independent ground truth: at least three floor marks with measured spacing and heading, one
   chosen as the map origin. T3.5e is only done when recovery is shown against those marks. **First check:** if the
   tape mark can be identified, measure the spin start position against it (distance along and across the mark,
   and heading). That is the first ground-truth test of the 2.3 m offset (1.94, 1.19 m).
4. Scope: version 1 = option A (decided above).

against ground truth beyond one tape-measured pose pair (error about 7 cm in plan, not a statistic); the 2.3 m offset is a LiDAR-geometry result and the three-floor-mark check has not been done. The package in step 3 was not run.
